Suggested moves

The editor's 💡 window extends a study by a greedy maximization of its expected score: at each step, it adds the one move of the studied side that raises the expected score most, until a number of moves has been added or a target score is reached. This page defines the objective, the set of admissible additions, the gain of an addition and the search for the best one, and states the method's limits. The command-line tool tools/optimal_repertoire.py implements the same method.

1. Objective

Let \(T\) be the study, a tree of moves rooted at its starting point. The studied side plays its prepared move in each position (the first child); the opponent plays each move \(m\) listed by the Opening Explorer in a position \(x\) with probability $$p_x(m) = \frac{n_x(m)}{\sum_{m'} n_x(m')},$$ where \(n_x(m)\) is the number of games in which \(m\) was played in \(x\). A line ends with its last answered move. The expected score is $$S(T) = \sum_{\ell \in L(T)} P(\ell)\, v(\ell),$$ where \(L(T)\) is the set of lines' ends, \(P(\ell)\) the product of the opponent's probabilities along the line and \(v(\ell) \in [0, 1]\) the value of its end. This is the expected score of the Stats page, with the end-of-prep value chosen in the editor's settings:

  • Explorer: \(v\) is the score of the line's last move over its Explorer games, \(v = (w + d/2)/n\), with \(w\), \(d\), \(n\) the wins, draws and games from the studied side's point of view.
  • Stockfish: \(v = \sigma(c)\), with \(c\) Stockfish's evaluation in centipawns of the position after the last move, from the studied side, and \(\sigma(c) = 1/(1 + e^{-0.00368208\,c})\).
  • Hybrid: the Explorer value when the move has at least 100 games, \(\sigma(c)\) otherwise.

Since the studied side's moves carry probability 1, \(S\) is linear in the value of each position: a position \(t\) where the opponent is to move, reached with probability \(r_t\) and of value \(S_t\), contributes \(r_t S_t\) to \(S(T)\).

2. Admissible additions

An addition is a pair \((m, a)\): an opponent move \(m\), in a position \(t\) of the study where the opponent is to move, that the study leaves unanswered, and a move \(a\) of the studied side answering it. The position \(t\) is either inside a line, or after a line's last move, which the addition then extends. The reply \(m\) is admissible if its probability of being reached is at least a threshold \(\rho\) (0.2% by default): $$r_t\, p_t(m) \ge \rho.$$ When the starting point is a position of the studied side with no move prepared, the only additions are the studied side's first moves there. With “After the selected move only”, an addition is admissible only if \(t\) is the selected node or follows it, or if \(m\) is the selected node; \(S\), \(r_t\) and the gains remain those of the whole study, from its starting point.

The candidates \(A(y)\) in the position \(y = t \cdot m\) are the studied side's moves listed by the Explorer, by decreasing number of games: the first \(k\) (6 by default) with at least \(n_{\min}\) games (100 by default) that satisfy the soundness constraint, if one is set. A move \(a\) is sound when it loses at most \(\Delta\) centipawns against Stockfish's best move: $$\hat c(y) - \hat c(y \cdot a) \le \Delta,$$ with \(\hat c\) Stockfish's evaluation from the studied side, at the depth chosen in the settings, clipped to \([-1000, 1000]\) so that a forced mate counts as 10 pawns; the evaluation of \(y\) stands for that of its best move. The levels offered are \(\Delta = 10\) (best only), 25 (excellent or better), 50 (good or better), 100 (up to inaccuracies), a value of the user's, or \(\Delta = \infty\) (any move, the default; Stockfish is then not needed for this).

3. Gain of an addition

A new move \(a\) ends its line. Its value \(\lambda(a)\) is the end-of-prep value of §1 for a line ending with \(a\). Let \(v_t(m)\) be the current value of the reply \(m\) in \(t\), that of a line ending with \(m\), and $$S_t = \sum_{m'} p_t(m')\, v_t(m')$$ the value of \(t\) with the study's current answers. Answering \(m\) with \(a\) replaces \(v_t(m)\) by \(\lambda(a)\). If no reply in \(t\) was answered, the line ended with the move before \(t\), with value \(e_t\), and now goes on through \(t\). By the linearity of §1, the gain is exact: $$G(m, a) = r_t \left[\, S_t - p_t(m)\, v_t(m) + p_t(m)\, \lambda(a) - S_t^{\text{old}} \right],$$ $$S_t^{\text{old}} = \begin{cases} e_t & \text{if the line ended before } t, \\ S_t & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$ For the first move at the starting point, \(G(a) = \lambda(a) - v_0\), with \(v_0\) the starting position's own value.

4. The search for the best addition

\(G\) is increasing in \(\lambda(a)\), so for each reply \(m\) only the candidate of largest \(\lambda\) needs to be considered. Since \(\lambda \le 1\), $$G(m, a) \le U(m) = r_t \left[\, S_t - p_t(m)\, v_t(m) + p_t(m) - S_t^{\text{old}} \right].$$ The bound \(U\) only needs the Explorer data of \(t\), whereas \(G\) needs that of \(y = t \cdot m\) (and Stockfish's evaluations, if required). The admissible replies are therefore examined by decreasing \(U\), and the search stops at the first reply whose bound does not exceed the best gain found. The result is the exact maximizer of \(G\) over all admissible additions, at the cost of the Explorer data of the replies examined only.

The best addition is applied to a working copy of the study, \(S\) is recomputed, and the step repeats. The procedure stops when \(N\) moves have been added, when \(S\) reaches the target, when the user stops it, or when no addition has a positive gain. The study itself is changed only when a suggestion is added.

5. Limits

  • Myopia. A move is valued as the end of its line, \(\lambda(a)\), not with the continuations that could follow it. A move whose own results are poor but whose follow-ups are strong is undervalued, and a pair of moves worth more together than the sum of their single-step gains can be missed. The method is therefore not optimal for a given number of moves; an exact solution, a knapsack over the tree computed from the leaves up, needs the Explorer data of every position within its limits, a number that grows exponentially with depth, instead of those next to the study.
  • Small samples. Explorer values are raw frequencies: a move's score over \(n\) games is uncertain by about \(\pm 50/\sqrt{n}\) points, ±5 at 100 games. The minimum \(n_{\min}\) limits the effect; the command-line tool can also shrink every score toward 50% as if \(n_0\) more games had been drawn (--prior-games), \(v = (w + d/2 + n_0/2)/(n + n_0)\).
  • Candidates. Only the \(k\) most played moves are candidates. The command-line tool can add Stockfish's best moves (--engine-candidates); the editor does not.
  • Stockfish's noise. \(\hat c(y)\) and \(\hat c(y \cdot a)\) come from two searches at a fixed depth, which can differ by a few tens of centipawns at depth 12: small values of \(\Delta\) are better used with depth 16.
