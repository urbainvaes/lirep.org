Stats

Screen states, calculation rules, data sources, and progress behavior for repertoire analysis.

Overview Studies Stats Practice

Purpose and access

Stats evaluate a saved study against a selected pool of real games. The studied side is assumed to remember and always play its prepared move. Opponent replies are weighted by their frequency in the selected Lirep or Lichess Explorer source.

The Stats page requires Lichess sign-in. Show a sign-in prompt when signed out and “Study not found” when a requested ID is unavailable to the account.

Study cards

  • The home page groups study cards by studied side. Each card shows the study name, opening moves, and expected score as a percentage (or a dash if not calculated).
  • Every card's Stats button opens the study's Stats page, including cards without a calculated score. Calculations are started from the Stats page; the cards have no Calculate/Recalculate button or job sequence.

Study detail screen

Open at stat.html?id={studyId}. From top to bottom: the header, the calculation settings, the expected score with its coverage chart, the expected evaluation, and practice knowledge.

  • Header: the tier badge (click it for the legend), study name, Playing White/Black badge, the custom starting point if one is set, and links to edit the study and to practice it. The tier is by expected score: gold at 60%+, silver at 55%+, bronze at 50%+, below breakeven under 50%.
  • Calculation settings: one panel holding every input of both calculations. Collapsed, it shows a one-line summary (e.g. “Lichess · 2000+ · blitz, rapid · Stockfish depth 16”) and an Edit button. Expanded, it shows the Opening Explorer settings, which weight the opponent's replies for both results, and the Stockfish depth (Quick 8, Balanced 12, Thorough 16), used for the expected evaluation only. Below it, Recalculate runs both calculations with the settings shown, one after the other, and a line says whether the results are up to date.
  • Expected score: the score as a percentage (a win = 1, a draw = 0.5, a loss = 0, from the studied side's point of view), the win/draw/loss split, the settings, position count and time it was calculated with, and the coverage chart.
  • Expected evaluation: a weighted Stockfish score in pawns from the studied side's point of view, with two decimals and a sign, or a forced-mate label for capped mate values; the settings, depth and time it was calculated with. If end positions lacked an engine evaluation, they contribute 0.00 and the card says how many. Older server-calculated summaries are marked as legacy until recalculated.
  • Practice knowledge: average recall across positions, or a note when there is no practice data, with a link to practice.

Each result has its own Recalculate button, shows “Not calculated yet” when empty, and shows two status lines:

  • Out of date, when the settings currently in the panel differ from the ones it was calculated with (Explorer settings, and the depth for the expected evaluation), or when the moves or starting point changed since. Changing a setting only marks results out of date; nothing recalculates until a Recalculate button is pressed. Moves are compared through a fingerprint of the tree (every move, in order, and the starting point; not comments) that the backend returns as movesFingerprint and stores with each result. Results saved before fingerprints existed are not flagged for moves.
  • Leaderboard status: its rank on the community leaderboard (among results from the same Explorer, so a result from the 2016 sample gets its rank in that view), or why it is not listed (study not shared, one player's games, depth below 12 or unknown, or outside the top ten).

Calculation definitions

Expected score and coverage

These are exclusively Opening Explorer-derived; never approximate them with engine scores. Walk from the effective starting node. At a studied-side turn, follow its sole prepared child. If preparation ends on the studied side's turn, use that position's Explorer W/D/L score as a neutral fallback. At an opponent turn, fetch that position's move list and weight each listed move by its game count divided by the total listed games. Recurse through prepared replies; for a reply absent from the tree, use that move's own W/D/L result from Explorer and stop that branch. A terminal result inside the tree is exact (win 1, draw .5, loss 0). If there are no Explorer games, use neutral expected score .5.

Coverage tracks probability mass that remains inside the tree under the same opponent move weights. Start at 1.0. Studied-side moves preserve mass; opponent moves split it by frequency; mass on an unprepared reply exits the book. Record the remaining mass after each opponent move. Thus coverage can only drop on opponent replies. The tooltip also reports the number of tree moves/branches represented at that depth, a structural count rather than a weighted count.

Expected evaluation

Use the same weighted tree walk and studied-side perfect-play assumption, but assign leaves an engine evaluation. When an opponent reply leaves the tree, evaluate the resulting position and weight it by that reply's real frequency. If a studied-side leaf or position with no usable Explorer games is reached, evaluate the current position. Checkmate is represented by ±100,000 centipawns; draws are 0. Store evaluations in White's POV, then convert to the studied side's POV for the result. Display cp as pawns (cp / 100).

Explorer move lists are capped by Lichess (roughly the top dozen moves), so rare omitted moves are not separately modeled. A custom start node changes the calculation root only; it does not trim or renumber the tree. Coverage move numbers are relative to that start node and the chart identifies which opponent move each point follows.

Calculations

The two results are calculated independently; each updates its own fields and timestamp without clearing the other. Recalculate runs both in turn; each result's own button runs it alone. All buttons are disabled while a calculation runs; on failure, the message shows beside the result's progress bar and the buttons come back. Both calculations save the settings they used as the study's Explorer settings.

Expected score

Runs in the browser (frontend/src/expectedScore.ts): the weighted tree walk for win/draw/loss probabilities and coverage, with Explorer data from the browser. It does not call Stockfish. Progress is estimated from the tree's size, because the number of positions is not known in advance. The results and the settings used go to POST /api/studies/{id}/expected-score, which checks their ranges and saves them with the moves fingerprint.

Explorer requests

Both calculations, and the Explorer panels, ask Lichess's Explorer directly from the browser with the user's own token (from GET /api/lichess-token, scope preference:read only), so each user's requests count against their own Lichess rate limit instead of all sharing the server's. Requests are paced: one at a time, at least 500 ms apart; after an HTTP 429, everything waits 60 seconds, then a calculation's request retries once while its progress label says so, and a panel's request fails at once with a wait-a-minute message. Panel requests go ahead of a calculation's queued ones. Responses are cached in the browser's IndexedDB for 30 days (24 hours for the player database). Since the browser calculates the results, the server cannot verify them beyond their ranges. See explorer-cache.md.

Expected evaluation

Runs in the browser. First discover the FENs at the frontier of the expected-evaluation walk, starting at the effective starting node: follow prepared moves and fetch opponent replies from the Explorer. The frontier includes nonterminal studied-side leaves, opponent positions with no listed games, and positions after unprepared opponent replies; terminal positions need no engine score. Reuse this account's cached FEN evaluations at the selected depth on this device and run local Stockfish only for missing FENs; results at different depths are cached separately. Store each completed White-POV centipawn value in browser IndexedDB immediately, so interrupted runs can resume. Live Study/Practice analysis keeps its fixed default depth.

Then perform the Explorer-weighted tree walk locally. A position without a usable engine score contributes 0.00 and is counted in evalMisses; there is no Cloud Eval fallback. Send evalCp, evalMisses, the Stockfish depth, and the selected explorerSettings to synchronous POST /api/studies/{id}/expected-eval. The backend saves the summary, the depth, the settings (as evalSettings, apart from the expected score's) and the moves fingerprint, not per-position evaluations. The page opens with the depth of the study's last expected evaluation, or this browser's last choice for a study never calculated.

The community page has a second leaderboard ranked by expected evaluation. It lists only evaluations at depth 12 (Balanced) or more, under the same Explorer rules as the score leaderboard: never one player's games, and one Explorer's results at a time. Both leaderboards rank Lichess's Explorer results by default; when some shared study has results from the local Lirep Explorer (the 2016 sample), an “Explorer data” switch (Lichess / 2016 sample) shows those ranked on their own, since the two aren't comparable. Evaluations saved before the depth was recorded are not ranked until recalculated.

Explorer settings

Each study saves its own Explorer settings, shown and editable on its Stats page and in the study page's Explorer panel. Expected score, coverage and expected evaluation are all calculated from the Explorer data these settings select, and the community leaderboards show, next to each result, the settings it was calculated with.

Data source

  • Lirep: Lirep's own copy of Lichess's open-source opening explorer, seeded with games from Lichess's public database (released under CC0). It currently holds the rated standard games from February and March 2016, about 10.7 million games. It has no rate limit, its responses are not cached because they never change, and it has no Masters database. The data is a subset: two months of mostly 2016-era play, so move frequencies may differ from today's. Lirep is offered only when the server has its local explorer configured.
  • Lichess: Lichess's hosted opening explorer, with its full and up-to-date databases. It is rate-limited and requires Lichess sign-in, so it is queried from the user's browser with their own token, paced and cached as described under Explorer requests.

Database

  • Players: games between Lichess players, filtered by rating and speed. Available from both sources.
  • Masters: over-the-board games by strong players (rated 2200 FIDE and above). Only from Lichess; it disables the rating and speed controls, which do not filter it.
  • Player: one named player's games as the studied side (like the Player tab of Lichess's explorer), defaulting to the signed-in account. Always queried from Lichess, whatever the source setting; it disables only the rating control. Scores calculated from it are not ranked on the community leaderboard, since they describe one player rather than the whole pool.

Rating and speed

  • For Players, select a fixed rating threshold; that bucket and every higher bucket are included. A new study's picker starts pre-selected at the signed-in player's own bracket (Reference rating order Rapid, Blitz, Classical; default 1500 if unrated), but it's a one-time default like any other saved setting, not a mode that keeps tracking the player's rating.
  • Bullet, Blitz, Rapid, and Classical checkboxes; default to Blitz, Rapid, Classical, and always require at least one speed.

When settings take effect

Changing the controls on the Stats page alone does not trigger calculation. The next expected-score or expected-evaluation calculation sends the selected settings and saves them as the study's new settings (not a temporary preview), so subsequent views and calculations use them.

Coverage chart and caching

Render a responsive line chart with percentage on the vertical axis and the studied opponent's move number on the horizontal axis. The chart stops at the last move still reached by at least 0.5% of games, so a deep tree doesn't end in a long flat line at zero. Hovering near the line shows opponent move number, coverage percentage, and number of remembered moves at that depth. If no coverage is stored, show an explanatory empty state and require recalculation to populate it.

Lichess Explorer responses are cached in the browser's IndexedDB, per browser profile, for 30 days (24 hours for the player database); local Lirep explorer responses are not cached. Per-user rating buckets persist in backend SQLite. Engine evaluations are keyed by username, FEN, and search depth in browser IndexedDB, per account and per device, not synced. Clearing browser data forces recomputation; when IndexedDB is unavailable (including private modes that block it), evaluations last only for the current page/tab visit. On loading a study, legacy server-side evals.byNode values are imported into the local FEN cache at the old default depth where absent. An older database may still contain the unused cloud_eval_cache table, but new databases do not create it. The /api/studies/{id}/evals and /api/eval-cache/lookup endpoints have been removed.

Interpretation and limits

  • Expected score reports game results, not engine quality. It can disagree with expected evaluation; this is intentional.
  • All results assume perfect memorization and one prepared move at each studied-side decision. They do not model mistakes by the user.
  • Opponent frequencies reflect the selected current pool, not move quality. Sparse data, capped move lists, and changes in current ratings affect estimates.
  • Local Stockfish is fixed-depth and runs on the user's device. Missing local evaluations contribute 0 and are reported; no Cloud Eval fallback is used.
  • Recalculation is manual because Explorer requests and local engine searches may take time; changed settings or moves only mark results out of date. Both calculations persist the selected Explorer settings.
