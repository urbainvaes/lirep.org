Lirep: the quantitative repertoire builder

Most repertoire tools help you remember your moves. Lirep also tells you how good your repertoire is, in numbers, against the moves real players choose, and helps you decide what to prepare next.

Measure your repertoire

  • Expected score: the score you would get on average if you always played your prepared moves and your opponents chose theirs like the players in the Lichess Opening Explorer. One number for your whole repertoire, from real games.
  • Expected evaluation: Stockfish's evaluation where your preparation ends, averaged over the lines your opponents actually play. It runs in your browser.
  • Coverage: the share of your games still in your preparation after each of your opponent's moves, to see where your preparation runs out.
  • Your own player pool: choose the rating band and time controls, and Lichess's Explorer or Lirep's own copy of it, which has no rate limit.

See the moves that matter

In the study editor, Stockfish's arrows show two things at once: the colour says how good a move is, compared with Stockfish's best move, and the thickness says how often it is played in the Explorer. Popular mistakes, the moves your opponents actually get wrong, stand out at a glance, and Stockfish also evaluates the popular moves its own top lines leave out.

Remember it, share it, compare it

  • Practice quizzes you on your own moves, line by line; each position's knowledge fades over time and comes back for review before you would forget it.
  • Community leaderboards rank shared openings by expected score or expected evaluation, and any shared opening can be imported into your studies in one click.
  • Games: attach master games or your own to a study, from PGN or Lichess links, and replay them.
  • Study files: export a study, with its games, and import it again, here or on another Lirep server.
  • Free software, signed in with your Lichess account: nothing new to create.

Similar tools

Lirep is one of several free and open-source tools for building and drilling an opening repertoire. Its unique features include:

  • Easy sign-in with your Lichess account.
  • New metrics to evaluate an opening, such as the expected score (win % plus half the draw %) of your whole repertoire, calculated assuming the opponent plays moves according to probabilities given by the Lichess opening explorer, as well as the expected evaluation when reaching the end of preparation.
  • Easy sharing of studies with the community, and a one-click import of someone else's opening.
